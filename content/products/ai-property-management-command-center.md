## How to use this pack

1. Pick the section matching this week's bottleneck — owner comms, tenant comms, listings, ads/video, or paperwork.
2. Copy the prompt into your tool (Copy.ai for written communications, Canva for design work, AdCreative.ai for vacancy ads, Synthesia for video scripts, Speechify for document triage).
3. Fill in every `[BRACKETED]` field before running — generic inputs produce generic content, and property management is a detail business.
4. Route every tenant- or owner-facing output through human review before it sends. AI drafts, you sign.

## Three rules before any prompt

**Rule 1 — Never paste tenant PII into a consumer AI tool.** Prompts below use property facts (unit type, rent band, dates, repair status), never names, Social Security numbers, income details, or protected-class characteristics. Before feeding anything to an AI tool, strip the personal data and check the vendor's data-retention terms.

**Rule 2 — Fair housing applies to AI output.** Anything a tool writes — listing descriptions, ad copy, screening language — is your responsibility under the Fair Housing Act and your state's landlord-tenant law. Describing the ideal resident ("perfect for young professionals," "great for families") rather than the property can constitute unlawful steering. Checklist #2 is the pre-publish screen; run it on every vacancy ad.

**Rule 3 — Never auto-send an owner-facing number.** AI drafts the letter; you verify the occupancy, delinquency, and dollar figures against your books before it leaves your outbox.

## Part 1 — Owner communications prompts (8 prompts)

### 1. Monthly owner update letter

```
Act as a property manager writing to a real estate investor. Write a monthly
owner update for [PROPERTY NAME/ADDRESS]. Portfolio facts (units, occupancy
this month vs last, rents collected, delinquency $, maintenance completed,
open maintenance items with status, upcoming renewals and turnover dates):
[PASTE FACTS]. Investor priorities: [CASH FLOW / WEALTH PRESERVATION /
DEPRECIATION & TAX]. Tone: concise, numbers-first, no fluff.
Output: (1) 3-sentence summary the owner can skim in 20 seconds, (2) body
paragraphs with the numbers, (3) one "what I'm watching next month" item.
End with a question that makes the owner feel managed, not billed.
```

### 2. Multi-property portfolio health snapshot

```
Act as a senior property manager. Convert these monthly numbers into a
one-page portfolio health snapshot for [OWNER/LLP NAME]: [PASTE PROPERTY
TABLE: property, units, occupancy %, avg rent, delinquency, open tickets].
Output: (1) portfolio-wide scorecard table (this month vs last month vs
same month last year), (2) the 3 properties that need attention with the
specific reason for each, (3) 2-3 sentences on portfolio trend. Flag every
property with delinquency above [X%] or occupancy below [Y%] in bold.
```

### 3. Maintenance escalation notice to owner

```
Act as a property manager who owns bad news. Write a maintenance escalation
notice for [PROPERTY/UNIT]: issue [DESCRIBE ISSUE], first reported [DATE],
current status [STATUS], estimated cost range [RANGE], my recommendation
[RECOMMENDATION], consequence of deferring [CONSEQUENCE, e.g. mold risk /
tenant claim / vacancy]. Tone: calm, factual, decision-ready — the owner
should be able to reply "approved" in one line. Include a short "why now"
paragraph that protects the relationship while asking for the money.
```

### 4. Delinquency status update

```
Act as a property manager updating an owner on rent collection. Delinquency
facts for [PROPERTY NAME]: [PASTE: tenants behind with days late and amounts
AS NUMBERS ONLY — no names, no unit identifiers with personal details].
Write an owner update covering: total past rent, collection actions already
taken and their dates, next actions under the lease and state law
[STATE, e.g. notice-to-pay timeline], realistic best/likely/worst outcomes
for this month's collections. Tone: professional and unemotional. Do NOT
advise on eviction strategy beyond what the management agreement covers —
end by recommending the owner consult counsel if [THRESHOLD] is breached.
```

### 5. Renewal season recommendation memo

```
Act as a property manager preparing renewal decisions. Facts for
[PROPERTY/PORTFOLIO]: [PASTE: lease expirations by month, current rents,
market rent data I have, turnover cost estimates, unit condition notes].
Write a renewal recommendation memo: units to renew early (and offer
language), units to hold for market review, units to turn (and budgeted
turnover cost per unit). Include a one-paragraph market-context note the
owner can repeat to their partners. Use only the numbers I provided —
if a number is missing, insert [CONFIRM] instead of estimating.
```

### 6. Turnover cost summary

```
Act as a property manager closing out a unit turn. Items for [UNIT]: make-
ready repairs [LINE ITEM COSTS], paint [COST], clean [COST], days vacant
[DAYS], lost rent at [MONTHLY RENT]. Write a turnover summary the owner
will accept: total cost table, cost per vacant day, how the previous
tenant's deposit was applied (state it factually, not as advice), and
two sentences on what we're doing differently next turn to cut the
timeline by [TARGET] days.
```

### 7. Annual owner review packet

```
Act as a property manager preparing an annual review. Facts for
[PORTFOLIO NAME]: [PASTE: 12-month occupancy, rent per unit, collections
%, major maintenance, capital items, renewal outcomes]. Build an annual
owner review packet: (1) year-in-review summary in 5 bullets an owner can
paste into a partnership letter, (2) performance table (planned vs actual
on the numbers I gave), (3) capital-needs section with a suggested
12-month reserve plan for [CAPEX ITEMS], (4) next-year goals I should
propose to this owner. Mark anything missing a real number as [CONFIRM].
```

### 8. New owner onboarding welcome letter

```
Act as a property manager welcoming a new owner client. Details: owner's
goals [GOALS], portfolio size [UNITS], my management agreement scope
[SCOPE], communication cadence I provide [CADENCE], what I need from
them in the first 30 days [REQUESTS: docs, access, tax forms]. Write a
```

## Checklists

The 10 checklists below run the week. Copy them into your task manager or print them. Every checklist assumes the pack's three rules: no tenant PII in AI tools, human review before send, verified numbers only.

### Checklist 1 — The weekly 90-minute workflow

- [ ] Same time slot every week booked on the calendar (before phones wake up)
- [ ] Property facts sheet updated (occupancy, delinquency, open tickets, vacancies, renewals)
- [ ] Owner block drafted (P1-P5) and numbers verified against the books
- [ ] Tenant queue cleared: every reply, notice, reminder drafted and human-reviewed
- [ ] Vacancy block: listing copy refreshed for each vacant unit (P17-P24)
- [ ] Creative block: ad copy and video scripts queued for anything at week 2+ market
- [ ] Paperwork block: document triage row completed for each new lease/HOA/vendor doc
- [ ] Ops block: dashboard built, delinquency ladder position confirmed per tenant file
- [ ] Fair-housing pre-publish check (Checklist 2) passed on every ad and listing going out this week
- [ ] Next week's queue written before closing the session

### Checklist 2 — Fair-housing pre-publish check (every ad, listing, and social post)

- [ ] Every sentence describes the property or the terms — none describe the ideal resident
- [ ] No age, family status, occupation, religion, or ability characterizations anywhere
- [ ] No "walking distance to" phrasing used as a lifestyle code — check each instance for intent
- [ ] Photos and AI-generated visuals show the property, not an implied ideal renter
- [ ] Application criteria stated identically for every unit (income source list, credit band, deposit)
- [ ] AI-generated copy read once end-to-end by a human who did not generate it
- [ ] Steering self-test passed: would this language read the same written by hand?
- [ ] Screening language matches what I actually apply — no stated criteria I do not enforce

### Checklist 3 — Owner communication cadence

- [ ] Monthly update sent by the same calendar day every month (day [X])
- [ ] Emergency escalation (P3) hand-delivered by phone first, written memorial after
- [ ] Delinquency update includes numbers, actions taken, next actions — never just mood
- [ ] Every owner-facing number re-verified against the books by a human
- [ ] Annual review packet scheduled before the owner asks for it
- [ ] Copies of every owner letter filed to the owner's folder with date stamps

### Checklist 4 — Unit turnover launch
