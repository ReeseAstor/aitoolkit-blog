## How to use this pack

1. Pick the section matching this week's bottleneck — reviews, posts/Q&A, photos, ranking content, or the citation audit.
2. Copy the prompt into your tool (Copy.ai or Jasper for written work, Canva for graphics, Surfer for ranking content, Speechify for reading material).
3. Fill in every `[BRACKETED]` field before running — generic inputs produce generic replies, and local buyers can smell a template.
4. Every review reply, question answer, and post is public-facing content on your most-visited sales page. Human review is not optional; it is the product.

## Three rules before any prompt

**Rule 1 — Never paste customer PII into a consumer AI tool.** Prompts below use business facts and reply-usable information only — never reviewer names, employees' personal details, phone numbers, or account specifics. Reviewers' names don't need to enter the AI at all: "the Tuesday dinner reviewer" is a complete reference. Before feeding anything, check the tool's data-retention terms.

**Rule 2 — Review integrity is a platform policy, a consumer law, and a trust asset.** Never write or seed fake reviews. Never offer, promise, or imply anything in exchange for a review (that's review-gating, against Google's policy). Never post a reply that references a dispute detail you wouldn't read aloud in the store. Review replies are your public voice, permanently attached to your listing — written to persuade the next reader, not just to answer the reviewer.

**Rule 3 — Never publish a claim you can't keep.** "Open until 9pm," "free parking," "financing available" — the AI will happily write whatever facts you give it, and Google's systems (and sometimes AI Overviews citing your Profile) will repeat them verbatim. Verify every operational fact against your live hours, services, and price list before the prompt output leaves the outbox.

## Part 1 — Review replies: the revenue job (8 prompts)

Six prompts write the replies; two generate the raw material. The reply block is first in the workflow because it's the highest-leverage 20 minutes of the whole session: most customers read owners' replies to reviews before choosing, and they read hardest for how a business responds to trouble.

### 1. The five-star review reply (varied, keyword-aware)

```
Act as the owner of [BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY/NEIGHBORHOOD]. Write a reply to a
five-star review. What the reviewer praised: [THEIR SPECIFIC PRAISE, e.g.
"fast turnaround / that specific person / a particular dish or service"].
Requirements: reply in 3 sentences or fewer; include the service/product phrase
"[YOUR PRIMARY GBP CATEGORY PHRASE, e.g. emergency plumbing in [CITY]]" once,
naturally; invite a specific next step only if it fits ([NEXT STEP OR "none"]).
Vary openings so consecutive replies don't share a first word — never open with
"Thank you". Warm, specific, not saccharine.
```

### 2. The mixed review (four stars, real complaint inside)

```
Act as a service manager writing a public reply to a mixed review for
[BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY]. The praise: [WHAT WENT WELL]. The complaint:
[WHAT WENT WRONG, e.g. wait time / communication gap / price surprise].
Write a public reply that: names the miss plainly in the first sentence
(no deflection); states one concrete thing changed since ([FIX]); offers to
continue the conversation offline with [CONTACT PERSON + CHANNEL] — never a
raw link. Tone: steady, un-defensive. Under 90 words. Do not mention that you
are replying to see a rating improved; write as the business, not as the score.
```

### 3. The one-star complaint

```
Act as the owner of [BUSINESS TYPE] addressing a one-star review publicly.
What happened from the business's side: [PASTE FACTS, no PII, no dispute
details you'd not say in person]. Write a public reply that: (1) opens with
the miss, not the defense; (2) gives one sentence of context ONLY if the
facts support it — no excuses longer than one sentence; (3) states what we
do when this happens ([INTERNAL STANDARD]); (4) invites [REDO / REFUND /
DIRECT CONTACT] on our initiative. Do not ask the reviewer to change or
delete their review anywhere in the reply. Under 110 words. Tone: the
manager a critical neighbor would want to deal with.
```

### 4. The unfair review (wrong business, misunderstanding, bad-faith)

```
Act as the owner of [BUSINESS TYPE] replying publicly to a review that
appears inaccurate: [WHAT THE REVIEW CLAIMS] vs [WHAT OUR RECORDS/FACTS
SHOW — numbers, dates, photos exist, no PII]. Write a calm public reply
that: states the specific fact once, verifiable ([E.G. "our sign-in log
shows no appointment for that date" / "we closed at 6pm, not 8pm — hours
are posted"]); avoids arguing the reviewer's character or motives; gives
the next reader the deciding detail; offers resolution if any real gap
exists. Do not threaten takedown action. Under 90 words. Tone:
unhurried, factual — you're winning over the reader, not the reviewer.
```

### 5. Review-request messages (no gating, policy-clean)

```
Act as a customer-experience lead at [BUSINESS TYPE]. Write 3 review-request
messages, each for a different moment: right after [MOMENT A, e.g. service
completion], next-day, and in the follow-up visit. Rules: identical message
for every customer (no conditional language that screens customers into
different requests); one direct link placeholder [YOUR GBP REVIEW LINK];
one specific memory hook each ("we hope [THE DETAIL THEY'D REMEMBER]"); no
reward, discount, or incentive anywhere. Each under 50 words, plain-text
ready. Then list the 3 moments where asking is most likely to catch [CUSTOMER
TYPE, e.g. a restaurant regular] at genuine satisfaction.
```

### 6. The photo prompt that produces reviews

```
Act as a local-marketing designer for [BUSINESS TYPE]. Write copy for an
in-store sign, a post-service email, and a receipt footer that each ask a
satisfied customer to review us on Google — same single message and link
[GMB REVIEW LINK] across all three (no gating). For each, add: a one-line
"why it helps us" in the voice customers believe ([E.G. "word of mouth is
our only advertising"]), and the exact [SCANNABLE QR or SHORT LINK]
placement. Local, honest, zero pressure. Under 25 words per placement.
```

### 7. Review mining → weekly post and photo calendar

```
Act as a content strategist. I've pasted 10 review excerpts (keywords,
phrases, recurring names of staff or menu items — PII already stripped):
[PASTE EXCERPTS]. Extract: the 5 most repeated customer phrases, the 3
things customers praise that our GBP description does not mention, and 1
review keyword we never use in our posts. Then propose 4 weekly GBP posts
that quote the customers' own words (with their phrasing, not fabricated
quotes) and 3 photo missions that capture the praised moments.
Format: table with Post idea / Review phrase used / Photo to take.
```

### 8. The response-time triage sheet

```
Act as a local business ops manager. From this week's review/queue data:
[PASTE: review date, rating summary, replied y/n, unanswered Q&A count,
days to reply for the last 10 replies], build a triage sheet that sorts
into: (a) reply today (fresh negative + any unanswered question older
than [X days]), (b) reply this week (fresh positive), (c) stale (>21
days — reply anyway, one line, the public record matters more than
timing), and (d) needs photo/proof attached. Include a suggested
per-bucket reply deadline and the one-line status I can paste to
[STAFF ROLE] each Monday.
```

## Part 2 — Posts, offers, events, and Q&A prompts (8 prompts)

The posts-and-Q&A block is the Profile's broadcast layer. Google treats a weekly or near-weekly posting cadence as a signal of a maintained business, and Q&A is pure conversion: unanswered questions sit on your listing looking like a business that leaves mail in the box.

### 9. The What's New post

```
Act as a local content marketer for [BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY/NEIGHBORHOOD].
This week's news: [WHAT'S ACTUALLY NEW — seasonal menu, new staff member,
new service line, extended hours, renovation]. Write a GBP What's New post:
hook line that names the neighborhood or occasion; 2-3 sentence body with
one specific detail (price, date, name of the thing); closing CTA
[CALL / BOOK / VISIT THIS WEEK]. Under 120 words. Include a button-label
suggestion from Google's allowed set (Book, Order online, Call, Learn more)
and an alt-ready photo description for the attached photo.
```

### 10. The seasonal trend-jack post

```
Act as a local marketing strategist for [BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY]. The
seasonal moment: [MONTH/HOLIDAY/WEATHER/LOCAL EVENT]. Write 3 GBP post
openers that connect the moment to what we sell ([TOP SERVICE OR PRODUCT])
without claiming anything we don't do. Pick the strongest and write the
full post: hook, 2 sentences of substance, CTA [TYPE]. Add a one-line
photo brief (what to shoot today). Local specificity beats cleverness —
name the season, the street, or the event exactly.
```

### 11. The limited-time offer post

```
Act as a small-business promotion writer. Offer facts: what [OFFER —
e.g. 15% off fall service calls], real end date [DATE], any honest
conditions ([CONDITIONS OR "none"]). Write a GBP offer post: headline
with the number, one sentence on who it's for, one sentence on the
condition, CTA + end date stated twice (body + closing line — Google
shows offers in a distinct card). Then a 2-line script for staff to
honor it consistently. Flag anything that risks sounding like a bait
offer, and rewrite to keep it honest.
```

### 12. The event post + event follow-up

```
Act as a community-events marketer for [BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY/
NEIGHBORHOOD]. Event: [WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, WHY LOCALS CARE]. Write (a)
the pre-event GBP post with logistics a stranger needs (parking, cost,
who it's for), (b) a same-week reminder post variant, (c) a 24-hour
post-event post that thanks attendees and pivots to [NEXT STEP —
booking, menu, next event]. Each under 90 words. Suggest one photo
per post and one question to seed in public Q&A about the event.
```

### 13. The seeded Q&A list (owner asks, owner answers)

```
Act as a local SEO specialist for [BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY]. My GBP
category phrase: [PRIMARY PHRASE]. My services: [LIST 3-5]. Generate
10 questions serious local buyers actually type or ask before
choosing [BUSINESS TYPE] — mixing: buying-decision questions (do
you..., how much does..., do you offer...), local-logistics questions
(parking, walk-ins, service area), and trust questions (are you
licensed, what's your process). For each, draft the owner answer in
first person, 2-3 sentences, naming our specifics ([FILL: real
facts]) and avoiding every prohibited claim. Mark the 3 to publish
first (highest decision weight). No duplicate intents; each question
unique in wording.
```

### 14. Answering the real questions already on the Profile

```
Act as the owner of [BUSINESS TYPE]. Public question(s) sitting
unanswered on my Profile: [PASTE QUESTION(S) VERBATIM]. For each,
write a first-person answer: direct answer sentence first; one
supporting detail with a real fact ([FILL]); no sales pitch; if the
answer is "it depends," state the deciding factor in one sentence
and invite a call without a link. 2-4 sentences each. Then give a
one-line rule for which future questions to answer within 24 hours.
```

### 15. The monthly Q&A refresh list

```
Act as a local SEO content planner. Last month's seeded questions:
[PASTE LIST]. This month's facts: [NEW SERVICE / SEASON / PRICE
CHANGE / STAFFING]. Generate 6 new questions (not overlapping last
month's wording) buyers will ask this month — tie at least 2 to the
new facts — each with a first-person owner answer of 2-3 sentences
using [REAL DETAILS]. Also flag: which old answers now contain stale
info (hours, prices, names) and what to update them to.
```

### 16. The "what we should be posting about" gap check

```
Act as a content auditor for a local business. Here are my last 8
GBP posts (titles + first lines): [PASTE]. Here are my categories
and services: [PASTE]. Score on three axes: (1) did posts rotate
across offer, event, update, and product types or repeat one type?
(2) how many contained a neighborhood/locality name? (3) how many
were supported by a photo taken this month? Output a table, then a
4-week post plan that fixes the biggest gap first, with one
Canva-graphic prompt idea per week.
```

## Part 3 — Photos, video, and visual identity (6 prompts)

The visual block keeps the Profile looking alive. Profiles die visually in two ways: stale photo order (Google ranks recent uploads) and one-shot stock-looking graphics. These prompts keep both fresh on a zero-photographer budget. Canva is the tool; Synthesia turns a written script into a 60-90 second walkthrough video for service businesses with no video staff.

```

colors [HEX CODES], brand fonts [FONTS]. Produce a design brief I can
type into Canva Magic Design: format (social post, 4:3 for GBP),
headline text (max 6 words, from this post: [PASTE POST HOOK]),
required text elements (headline, date/offer line, logo corner), and
the photo to feature ([WHAT PHOTO]). Keep 60% clean space; text
readable at phone size. Output the brief plus a 3-variant B-roll
instruction list (what to type/select in Canva for each).
```

```
Act as a local photography director. Business: [TYPE + LOCATION].
What the Profile currently shows: [DESCRIBE: count, recency, types].
Generate 8 photo missions for this month — each: what exactly to
shoot, best time of day, phone-camera framing tip, and the GBP feature
it feeds (post, photo, cover, video). Mix: team faces, product/
service close-ups, premises exterior+interior, process shots, and
one community moment. Prior 2 with fastest upload (the ones to shoot
today) and note: shoot 2:1 backups of each for post-crop reuse.

## Part 4 — The ranking content layer (8 prompts)

The Profile ranks on its own merits, but the pages behind it decide how much surface area local search has to grab. Google's local ranking help documentation has always pointed at "prominence" — and in practice that means links and content authority the Profile alone can't carry. In 2026 this layer has a second customer: the AI search layer that synthesizes local overviews from Profiles, reviews, and authoritative local pages.
### 23. The service-page brief (Surfer)

```

[CITY]" for [BUSINESS TYPE]. Competitor URLs Google ranks above us:
[PASTE 3]. Generate a Surfer-ready content brief: primary keyword
variant set for the H1 and first 100 words; H2 outline (services
within the service, process/steps, trust elements, local proof,
FAQ); entities and local modifiers the brief should include; target
word count based on the current top-3 average; internal links to
[EXISTING PAGES]. Mark the 3 sections a [NAMED COMPETITOR] page is
missing entirely — those are our differentiation paragraphs.
```

### 24. The location-page brief (multi-area service business)

NEIGHBORHOODS/SUBURBS]. Write a location-page brief template I can
reuse: the 5 unique content blocks each area variant must contain
(landmarks/communities served, area-specific service examples, one
real job story if available, area logistics like parking/hours,
distinct testimonial), the local keyword phrases to rotate per area,

## Part 5 — The AI-search layer and brand-voice governance (10 prompts)

The last block is what changed in 2026. AI search now sits between the customer and the map pack: Google's AI Overviews answer local queries from a synthesis of Profiles, reviews, and authoritative local pages, and AI answers are where a growing share of local discovery resolves. The first four prompts run the citation audit — how AI describes you today; the rest keep a multi-location operation on brand voice at scale. Jasper's shared brand-voice feature is the multi-location tool; Speechify is the reading layer for the material you'd otherwise never get through.

### 31. Ask the AIs what they say about you (citation audit)

```
Act as an AI-visibility researcher for a local business. Business:
[BUSINESS TYPE + NAME PATTERN (no exact PII needed)] in [CITY]. For
ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews separately,
generate the exact test prompts I should run on the first of each
month: (1) "best [CATEGORY] in [CITY]" straight query; (2) the
comparison query vs our main competitor [COMPETITOR NAME]; (3) the
situational query our best customers actually have ([EXAMPLE, e.g.
"emergency [SERVICE] on a Sunday in [CITY]"]); (4) a
recommendation-detail query ("does [BUSINESS TYPE] in [CITY]
[OFFER/ATTRIBUTE]?"). For each test: what to record (cited or not,
quoted facts, sentiment), and the Profile field most likely to feed
each answer. Output as a reusable monthly checklist with a results
table template.
```

### 32. Diagnose why the AI answer isn't you

```
Act as a GEO (generative engine optimization) analyst. The AI
overview for "[CATEGORY] in [CITY]" cites [WHAT IT DOES CITE —
competitors, directories, review sites] and describes our business
as [WHAT IT SAYS, VERBATIM IF WRONG]. Our assets: GBP [STATUS:
posts cadence, photos, Q&A], site pages [PASTE LIST], review
profile [COUNT + RATING + RECENCY]. Diagnose the 3 most probable
causes ranked by fix speed: extractability (is the defining fact
stated plainly anywhere?), profile freshness (post/photo recency vs
the sites it cites instead), and third-party corroboration (which
directories/review platforms the answer pulls from). For each
cause: the specific fix, the tool that does it ([Copy.ai/Jasper for
content, Canva for fresh visuals]), and how fast it plausibly
changes the answer.
```

### 33. Build the fix list and rewrite for extractability

```
Act as a content editor optimizing for AI citations. Take my main
service description as it currently exists: [PASTE CURRENT
DESCRIPTION FROM GBP + WEBSITE]. Rewrite it in two versions: (1)
the extractable version — a 40-60 word block that states WHO we

## The 10 checklists (one-page production screens)

Use these as the weekly pre-publish screens. A checklist earns its place by catching a real failure mode — each one below exists because that failure happens weekly in real Profiles.

### C1 — The weekly triage sheet (Minutes 0-10)

- [ ] Export/pull this week's new reviews (count, ratings, any negatives flagged)
- [ ] List unanswered public questions with age in days
- [ ] Note overdue reply (>72h) and overdue question (>24h) on top of the list
- [ ] Pull 1-2 photo opportunities spotted this week (delivery, before/after, team)
- [ ] Check last week's post performance (views on each)
- [ ] Write the one-line triage decision: reply block first or posts block first?

### C2 — Every review reply (run before each reply sends)

- [ ] Reply names the specific thing the review names (not a generic thanks)
- [ ] Reviewer's name/personal details do NOT appear — the reply works without PII
- [ ] No dispute detail, staff-discrimination language, or blame-shifting
- [ ] Negative replies offer a direct human contact (person + channel, not a bare link)
- [ ] Positive reply includes the category phrase naturally (once max)
- [ ] Reads aloud as a human voice; no AI-template opener repeated from last reply
- [ ] The reply would make the NEXT reader feel safe choosing us

### C3 — Review generation (weekly ask)

- [ ] This week's ask moment identified (completion/note/email/receipt)
- [ ] Single identical message for all customers (no gating language)
- [ ] Link/QR tested on a phone this week (it resolves to the correct listing)
- [ ] No incentive, discount, or reward offered or implied
- [ ] Ask count logged (we track asks vs reviews to keep the funnel honest)

### C4 — Every post before it goes live

- [ ] One post per week minimum (two max, spaced at least 3 days apart)
- [ ] Post type matches the week's plan (offer/event/update/photo rotation)
- [ ] Names city/neighborhood naturally at least once
- [ ] Contains one specific detail (number, date, name, price) — no pure-adjective posts
- [ ] Facts verified live (hours, price, dates) — nothing the staff can't honor
- [ ] Photo attached, and it's a real photo (no repeated stock image twice in a row)
- [ ] Button matches the action (Book / Call / Learn more / Order)
- [ ] Proofread: no doubled spaces, no "[BRACKET]" placeholder left in, no truncated sentence

### C5 — Q&A management

- [ ] All public questions aged >24h answered this week
- [ ] Seeded Q&A: 1-2 new pairs published monthly (asked AND answered by us, honestly labeled)
- [ ] No answer contains a prohibited claim (guarantees, superiority language)
- [ ] Upvoting natural questions only — never mass-upvote seeded items
- [ ] Every answer reads first-person owner voice; no third-party corporate speak

### C6 — Photos and video

- [ ] At least one new photo uploaded this week (recency check passes)
- [ ] Cover/logo checked once a month (C6 runs monthly for this line)
- [ ] Caption names city/neighborhood naturally
- [ ] No photo contains: customers who didn't consent, staff faces without permission, competitor signage, license plates, or children without guardian consent
- [ ] Video (if posted): 60-90s, captioned, no copyright audio
- [ ] The photo set would convince a stranger this is an ACTIVE business — not a stock catalog

### C7 — Ranking-content alignment (weekly 15-minute pass)

- [ ] This week's content block selected the page brief (P23/P24/P25) if queue permits
- [ ] Any drafted page's H1 carries the [service + city] phrase the Profile category matches
- [ ] Answer-block (P26) added/refreshed on at least one live page monthly
- [ ] GBP description and website first paragraph tell the same story (spot check)
- [ ] At least one page links back to the GBP-attached site domain this week (reciprocal check)

### C8 — Multi-voice consistency check (multi-location, weekly)

- [ ] All location posts this week share the saved Jasper Brand Voice descriptor
- [ ] Local flex items (landmarks, events, staff names) present per location post
- [ ] Zero unauthorized offers/policy claims from any location
- [ ] No two locations' posts open with the same sentence
- [ ] Regional manager validation checklist (from P35) completed for every location
- [ ] Flagged exceptions (missing inputs) held for manual review — not auto-published

### C9 — The monthly citation audit (AI-search layer)

- [ ] The 4 AI test queries (P31) run and screenshotted/logged — all four engines
- [ ] Cited/not-cited status recorded with date in the tracking sheet
- [ ] Any wrong fact about us quoted by an AI flagged on the fix list (P32 diagnosis)
- [ ] The extractable description block (P33) live on GBP + website + top directory
- [ ] Corroboration sweep (P34) run this month; mismatches submitted for fix
- [ ] One citable asset published (service page refresh, answer block, or photo-rich post)

### C10 — The monthly performance review (30 minutes)

- [ ] Pull calls, direction requests, website clicks from GBP performance view
- [ ] Compare vs last month, note the number that moved most
- [ ] Attribute to a specific action taken (post cadence, review replies, new photos)
- [ ] Record which post type got best engagement — repeat it next month
- [ ] Check response rate: % of reviews replied, median reply time
- [ ] Decide next month's single test (one variable only)
- [ ] Log the one-line verdict in the tracking sheet: keep, adjust, or escalate

## FAQ

**Is this a course?** No — it's production material. 40 prompts and 10 screens you copy, fill, and run. No videos to watch, no dashboard to log into.

**Will this replace my agency?** Not automatically. If your agency is writing your review replies and posts today, this pack does that production in-house in 90 minutes a week — many owners keep the agency for its judgment and take production back. But if the agency's whole retainer is GBP production, the pack does that job for under a coffee budget.

**Do the prompts work with ChatGPT instead of the named tools?** Yes — every written prompt runs in any capable assistant. The named tool matters for where the work lives afterward: Jasper's saved Brand Voice for multi-location consistency, Surfer's score for the ranking-content optimization pass, Canva's brand kit for graphics.

**How is this different from a "local SEO checklist"?** A checklist tells you what's broken. This is the production system that fixes it weekly — replies, posts, answers, graphics, page briefs, and a monthly audit of what AI search says about you.

**What if I have one location, not ten?** Skip the multi-location prompts (P35-P36, C8). The core weekly loop — reviews, posts, Q&A, one photo, one content move — still uses 20+ prompts.

**Does Google punish AI-written posts?** Google's guidelines for representing your business require that content is accurate and honest; they don't forbid drafting assistance. What violates trust and policy is fake reviews, bait offers, and claims your staff can't honor. Every prompt here drafts; you verify, edit, and publish under your name.

### Tool notes and honest framings

**Copy.ai** — the review-reply and posting engine for a single location. Chat pricing at $24/mo annual with 5 seats included is unusually generous: a two-person shop runs the whole product on one subscription. Start free; upgrade when the weekly session is a settled habit.

**Jasper** — worth the jump from Copy.ai only when you manage multiple locations with different hands posting. Its shared Brand Voice feature is the whole point: save the descriptor once in P35, and every franchisee drafts inside it. One-seat regional editors are the buyers; per-location seats are waste.

**Canva** — no-brainer tier. Keep the folder structure (one folder per location, subfolders per month) and the brand kit makes even rushed posts look consistent. Free tier covers everything in this pack; Pro matters if you also run social beyond the Profile.

**Surfer** — the only tool here with real scope risk. Its restructured tiers (Discovery $49 / Standard $99 / Pro $182, all billed yearly) are cheaper than the 2025 plans, but it is a content-operations platform, not a button. If you won't publish service pages monthly, buy nothing — use the brief-style prompts (P23-P25) in Copy.ai and skip the score-driven workflow. If you will publish monthly, Discovery pays for itself against one outranked competitor page.

**Speechify** — optional, and honestly labeled as such. It earns its slot for operators who spend windshield time listening to audit readouts (P38's one-page version), employee survey verbatims, or long competitor pages. If you don't have reading backlog, skip it.
