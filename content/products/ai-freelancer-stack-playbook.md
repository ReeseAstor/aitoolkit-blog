## 1. Why Freelancers Need a *Stack*, Not a Tool

The mistake most freelancers make in 2026 is buying tools in the same order they read about them:

1. Subscribe to ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo) "for research"
2. See a Copy.ai ad, sign up for the $49 Pro tier
3. Read a Synthesia case study, drop $89/mo for an avatar
4. Hear about AdCreative.ai on a podcast, add $59/mo
5. Get a Speechify annual plan because the Black Friday deal was good

Six months later you're at $237/month, you still wrote your last proposal in Google Docs, and you don't remember which tool is supposed to do what.

**A stack is a system.** You assign one tool to each function, you don't double-pay, and you know which button to press when a new lead lands. This playbook builds that.

### The two tests every tool must pass

Before we put any tool in the stack, it has to pass two tests:

- **Job test** — Does it do *one* job we need done, better than the free alternative?
- **Economics test** — Will it pay for itself in month 1, or month 2 at the latest?

If a tool fails either test, it doesn't go in the stack. Period.

## 3. Function 1 — Land Clients (writing + outreach)

**Job:** Turn cold leads into signed contracts. You need:
- Cold email sequences that don't sound like cold email
- LinkedIn DMs that earn a reply
- Proposals that close above $5k
- Follow-up messages that don't feel like nagging

**Tool pick: Copy.ai** (recommended) — or Writesonic on a budget.

### Why Copy.ai for landing work

- Has dedicated templates for cold email, follow-up, LinkedIn outreach, and proposals
- Workflows let you build a 5-step sequence once, then re-run it for every new lead
- Brand voice memory means your second email sounds like the first
- 2026 Chat tier is $24/mo annual ($29 monthly) — Pro tier $49/mo annual (was $49/mo) — see Appendix for the latest

### When to swap in Writesonic

If you write more SEO blog content for your own site than you do client outreach, Writesonic's SEO mode + bulk writer is a better fit. Bulk-generate 10 article briefs in one workflow. 2026: Standard is $20/mo annual, Professional $99/mo annual.

### Cold email prompt (Copy.ai)

```
You are a freelance [YOUR ROLE, e.g. "brand designer"] writing a 4-email cold
outreach sequence to a [TARGET, e.g. "B2B SaaS head of marketing"]. The
prospect recently [TRIGGER, e.g. "launched a new product line on LinkedIn"].

Constraints:
- Email 1: 60-90 words. One specific observation about their work, no flattery.
  Soft CTA: "Worth a 15-min call?"
- Email 2 (sent 4 days later, no reply): 50-70 words. Share a 1-paragraph
  relevant case study. CTA: same.
- Email 3 (sent 7 days later, no reply): 30-50 words. "Closing the loop" tone.
  CTA: "Should I assume the timing's off?"
- Email 4 (sent 10 days later, no reply): 25-40 words. Final breakup email.
  CTA: "If timing's better later, just reply 'later'."

Tone: direct, specific, no "I hope this email finds you well."
Avoid: exclamation points, emojis, and the word "just."

For each email, output: subject line, preview text, body.
```

### Proposal prompt (Copy.ai)

```
Write a freelance project proposal for [CLIENT NAME] based on these notes:

DISCOVERY CALL NOTES:
- Client pain: [paste 3-5 bullet points]
- Desired outcome: [1 sentence]
- Budget they hinted at: $[X]
- Timeline they mentioned: [X weeks]
- Decision-maker: [name, title]

MY POSITIONING:
- I help [AUDIENCE] achieve [OUTCOME] via [METHOD].
- Past work: [paste 2 relevant wins with metrics]

Deliver a 1-page proposal in this structure:
1. "Here's what I heard" (3 bullets — proof you listened)
2. "What I'd do" (3-5 phases, each with a deliverable)
3. "Investment" (3 pricing tiers: Starter, Standard, Premium)
4. "Timeline" (table)
5. "Next step" (single CTA: book a 30-min scope call)

Tone: confident, not salesy. No exclamation points. No "I'd love to."
```

### LinkedIn DM prompt

```
Write a 4-message LinkedIn outreach sequence for a freelance [ROLE] targeting
[TITLE] at [COMPANY TYPE].

Constraints:
- Connection request note: 280 characters max. Reference a specific post or
  recent company news. No "I'd love to connect."
- DM #1 (after they accept): 50-80 words. One specific observation, one
  question.
- DM #2 (3-4 days later, no reply): 40-60 words. Share a relevant case
  study in 2 sentences.
- DM #3 (7 days later, no reply): 30-40 words. "Should I assume the
  timing's off?" breakup.

Avoid: "Hope you're well," "Circle back," "Synergy," "Touch base."
```

## 4. Function 2 — Deliver Work (research, drafting, design, video)

**Job:** Produce the actual client deliverable. For most freelancers this is some mix of:
- Research (market, audience, competitors)
- Long-form writing (articles, reports, scripts)
- Visual design (social graphics, decks, mockups)
- Short-form video (Reels, TikTok, ads)

**Tool picks:**
- **Canva** for design and decks (Pro tier)
- **Synthesia** for video (no camera needed)
- **Speechify** for voice (narration, audio versions)
- **ChatGPT or Claude** for research and long-form (free tier is fine for most freelancers)

### The 80/20 of design: Canva

You will produce 80% of your visual work in Canva. Pro is $15/mo monthly, $120/yr annual — and unlocks:
- Brand kit (your logo, colors, fonts auto-applied)
- Background remover
- 100M+ stock photos and videos
- Magic Resize (one design → 7 sizes)
- Content Scheduler (post directly to 8 platforms)

For decks specifically, Canva's presentation mode is now competitive with Google Slides + Beautiful.ai for clients who don't need heavy data viz.

### When you need video: Synthesia

If the deliverable is a 60-second product explainer, a course intro, or a LinkedIn video that doesn't show your face, Synthesia at $29/mo (Starter) gives you 10 minutes of video/month with 230+ AI avatars in 140+ languages. $89/mo Creator plan doubles the minutes and unlocks custom avatars.

For client work: use Synthesia to draft a *v0* of the video, get client feedback, then re-render. Cuts the round-trip from 3 weeks to 3 days.

### Voice narration: Speechify

If the deliverable is an audio version of a blog post, a podcast intro, or a YouTube voiceover and you don't want to record, Speechify's AI voice studio at $139/yr is competitive. The Studio tier at $24/mo gives you commercial-use voices for client work — important, because the free tier is personal-use only.

### Research prompt (use with ChatGPT or Claude)

```
You are a research analyst for a freelance project. I'm a [YOUR ROLE] working
with [CLIENT TYPE] on [PROJECT TYPE].

I need:
1. Five recent (last 12 months) developments in [INDUSTRY] that affect my
   client's positioning.
2. Three direct competitors of [CLIENT] and how they position against this
   trend.
3. Two contrarian takes — what smart practitioners are saying is *wrong* about
   this trend.
4. One specific stat or data point I can cite (with source URL).
5. A 200-word "State of the Market" summary I can use as a section opener in
   the deliverable.

Cite your sources. If you don't know, say "I don't know" — don't make it up.
```

## 5. Function 3 — Bill and Get Paid (invoicing, contracts, expense tracking)

**Job:** Get paid on time, with airtight contracts, and without a bookkeeper.

**Tool picks (all free or under $20/mo):**

- **Bonsai** ($17/mo Solo) — proposals, contracts, invoicing, time tracking, taxes, all in one
- **Wave** (free) — invoicing + accounting for sole proprietors
- **Stripe** (free + 2.9% per transaction) — payment processing
- **Google Workspace** ($7/mo Starter) — email + drive + docs on a custom domain

### Recommendation: Bonsai if you bill >$3k/month, Wave if you bill less

Bonsai's contract templates alone are worth $17/mo — they cover scope creep, late payment, and IP ownership in plain English. Wave is unbeatable for pure invoicing if you have a simple LLC.

### Late-payment email prompt

```
Write a 3-email late-payment sequence for an outstanding invoice.

Invoice details:
- Client: [NAME]
- Project: [NAME]
- Amount due: $[X]
- Original due date: [DATE]
- Days overdue: [N]

Email 1 (1-7 days overdue): friendly reminder, 40-60 words, attach invoice
Email 2 (8-21 days overdue): firmer reminder, 60-80 words, mention late fee
Email 3 (22+ days overdue): final notice, 80-120 words, mention small-claims
option

Tone: professional, not apologetic. Never "just checking in."
```

**Job:** Turn completed projects into inbound leads. Most freelancers underinvest here because the work is invisible until it isn't.

**Tool picks:**

- **Canva** for LinkedIn carousel graphics

- **Buttondown** or **beehiiv** (free tier) for newsletter

### LinkedIn carousel prompt

```
Write a 7-slide LinkedIn carousel on [TOPIC] for a freelance [ROLE].

Target reader: [AUDIENCE]
Goal of the carousel: [EDUCATE / GENERATE LEADS / ANNOUNCE A FRAMEWORK]

Slide 1 (Cover): 8-10 words max. Pattern interrupt + benefit.
Slide 2 (Problem): 15-25 words. Stating the pain clearly.
Slide 3-5 (Insight): 15-25 words each. One specific idea per slide.
Slide 6 (Proof or example): 20-30 words. A mini case study.
Slide 7 (CTA): 10-15 words. Single next step ("DM me X" / "Comment
'guide' for the PDF").

For each slide, give: a headline, 2-3 supporting bullet points, and a
suggested Canva layout (text-heavy, stat callout, quote callout, etc.).
```

### Newsletter issue prompt

```
Write a 600-word newsletter issue on [TOPIC] for a freelance [ROLE]
audience.

Sections:
- "One thing I learned this week" (200 words, personal + tactical)
- "A resource worth 5 minutes" (150 words, link to a tool or article)
- "A question I'm sitting with" (150 words, invite replies)
- "If you only do one thing this week" (100 words, single specific action)

Voice: first person, conversational, short sentences. No "Hey fam," no
emojis except where they add meaning. Sign off with my name and a one-line
PS.
```

## 7. Function 5 — Reuse Everything (repurpose, SOPs, productized offers)

**Job:** Turn one project into five assets and one offer. This is the function that turns a freelancer into a business.

**Tool picks:**
- **Copy.ai** for repurposing long-form → social
- **Canva** for repurposing graphics → carousels → thumbnails
- **Gumroad** (free) for the productized offer
- **Notion** (free) for SOP storage

### Repurposing prompt

```
Take this [BLOG POST / CASE STUDY / PODCAST TRANSCRIPT] and produce 10
distinct assets:

1. 5 LinkedIn posts (different hooks, same core insight)
2. 3 Twitter/X threads (7 tweets each, hook-first)
3. 1 newsletter issue (600 words)
4. 1 YouTube Shorts script (45-60 seconds, hook in first 3 seconds)
5. 1 Instagram carousel outline (7 slides)
6. 1 lead magnet title + 3-bullet promise
7. 1 cold email pitch to promote the lead magnet
8. 1 podcast guest pitch email
9. 3 quote graphics (text + suggested Canva layout)
10. 1 sales page outline (problem → solution → proof → offer → CTA)

Keep the core insight consistent. Vary the format and the entry hook.
Don't rephrase the same paragraph 10 times.
```

### SOP prompt (for your own operations)

```
You are an operations consultant. I'm a freelance [ROLE] and I want to
document how I do [TASK] so a future subcontractor or VA can run it
without me.

Inputs:
- Task: [TASK NAME]
- Tools used: [LIST]
- Trigger: [WHAT STARTS THIS TASK]
- Output: [WHAT THE CLIENT GETS]
- Time to complete: [N hours]

Write a Standard Operating Procedure with:
- Goal (1 sentence)
- Inputs checklist
- Step-by-step instructions (numbered, each step one action)
- Decision points (if X, do Y; if Z, do W)
- Output checklist
- Common mistakes and how to fix them
- Estimated time per step

Format: ready to paste into Notion.
```

## 8. The 30-Day Rollout Plan

**Day 1-3: Audit and unsubscribe**

Print the Stack Audit worksheet (page 30). List every AI tool you currently pay for. For each, answer:
- Which of the 5 functions does it serve?
- What's the cheapest alternative in that function?
- When did I last use it for a paid deliverable?

Unsubscribe from anything that fails the Job test (section 1) or that you haven't used in 30 days. Save the receipts.

**Day 4-7: Pick your stack**

For each of the 5 functions, pick ONE primary tool using the recommendations in sections 3-7. Set up billing. Total monthly cost should land between $60-90.

**Day 8-14: Build your prompts**

For each function, save the prompts from this playbook into a "Prompt Library" Notion page. Customize them with your role, voice, and offer. Test each prompt on a real past project before going live.

**Day 15-21: Test on a real client**

Pick one active (or upcoming) project. Use ONLY the new stack for:
- The proposal (Function 1)
- One deliverable (Function 2)
- The invoice (Function 3)

Track time saved vs. your last similar project. Goal: 30%+ time saved on at least one function.

**Day 22-30: Repurpose and document**

Take the deliverable from Day 15-21 and run it through the Function 5 repurposing prompt. Generate 3-5 assets. Write one SOP for the part of the work you most want to outsource.

By Day 30, you should have:
- A stack of 5-7 tools you actually use
- A prompt library tuned to your voice
- One SOP ready to hand to a subcontractor
- A week's worth of repurposed content in the bank

## 9. The "Don't AI This Yet" List

These are the 7 things that will hurt your reputation if you let a model touch them without heavy editing:

1. **Your pricing in a proposal.** Always set your own price. AI will lowball you.
2. **A client's confidential data.** Never paste unredacted client docs into a public model.
3. **Anything that will be legally binding.** Contracts, NDAs, SOWs — use Bonsai templates, not raw AI.
4. **Personal health, medical, or financial advice to a client.** Refer them to a pro.
5. **The first draft of a reference or testimonial request.** It reads as a template — and they know.
6. **A "personal" apology email.** A model can't feel sorry. The asymmetry is obvious.
7. **Anything that names a specific person at a client organization without their consent** (e.g., a case study). Get the OK first.

## 10. Pricing Calculator — What to Charge for AI-Assisted Work

The wrong question: "Should I charge less because AI is faster?"

The right question: "Am I charging for time, or for outcome?"

If you charge for time, AI will eat your margins. **Charge for outcome.**

### Hourly → Value-based pricing rules

| Tier | Hourly floor | Project floor | What the client gets |
|------|--------------|---------------|----------------------|
| Solo freelancer (new) | $75/hr | $1,500 | One defined deliverable, 1 round of revisions |
| Established freelancer | $150/hr | $5,000 | Scoped project, 2 rounds of revisions, async updates |
| Specialist / niche expert | $300/hr | $15,000 | Strategy + execution, weekly calls, 3 rounds of revisions |
| Advisory / fractional | $500/hr | $30,000 | Embedded part-time, monthly retainer, white-glove |

**The AI trap:** If a client asks "but AI does this for free," respond with: "AI does the *draft* for free. You're paying me for the *judgment* — the part that knows which draft to ship, what to leave out, and how to make it sound like you."

### Discount policy

If a client says "you used AI, so it should be cheaper," offer a 10% discount on the *first* project only, with a note: "This is a one-time introduction to working together." Don't repeat it. Clients who will only pay less because of AI are not your clients.

## 14. When to Add a 6th Tool (and When Not To)

**Add a tool when:**
- You're using your current tool 5+ times a week and hitting a clear wall
- A new tool will pay for itself in 1-2 months of saved time
- The new tool replaces 2+ existing subscriptions (consolidation, not addition)

**Don't add a tool when:**
- A Black Friday deal tempts you
- A podcaster you admire mentions it
- Your friend says "you have to try this"
- It's a "platform" that wants to be your homepage (Notion, ClickUp, etc. — pick one, stop switching)

The default answer is **no**. A freelance stack is a discipline.
