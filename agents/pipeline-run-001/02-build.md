── PAGE 03 · Quick-Start Guide ──────────────────────────
Layout   : Numbered steps, single column
Callout  : [Pro Tip] Do this once a month, ideally on the last Sunday of
           the previous month. Block 90 minutes. Do not skip the brain dump.
Heading  : Your first 10 minutes.
Steps    :
  1. Open the Brain Dump (P4). Set a 10-minute timer. Write every content
     idea you have — titles, topics, half-formed thoughts — one per row.
     Do not filter. Fill as many rows as you can.
  2. When the timer ends, go to the Batch Day Planner (P6). Group similar
     ideas onto the same batch day (all talking-head videos on Day 1, all
     voiceover on Day 2, etc.).
  3. Pick your 4–8 strongest pieces. Open a Content Piece Tracker for each
     one (P9–P16). Fill in the title, platform, hook, and CTA fields only.
     Come back to the rest when you're ready to create.
  4. Put your batch dates in your calendar. You are done planning.
Callout  : [Note] You do not need to fill every page. Use what helps.
           Leave the rest blank.

── PAGES 23–25 · Prompt Block Library ───────────────────
Layout   : Card-style prompt blocks (3 per page)
Typography: Courier New / Consolas · Navy Slate background · Off White text

PAGE 23 — Ideas & Hooks (10 prompts):
  > Generate 10 video/post titles for [topic] that answer a common
    beginner question. Audience: [describe]. Tone: direct, no clickbait.

  > Write 5 hook lines for a video about [topic]. Each hook should
    start with a specific situation, not a question. Tone: calm, direct.

  > List 8 content ideas for [niche] that I have not covered yet,
    based on these titles I have already made: [paste list].

  > What are the top 3 objections someone has before buying [product
    or service]? Turn each objection into a content angle.

  > Write a "story hook" opening for a video about [topic]. The viewer
    should recognize themselves in the first sentence.

  > Generate a 30-day content calendar for [niche] covering: tutorials,
    opinion pieces, behind-the-scenes, and product-adjacent content.
    Format as a simple table.

  > What questions do beginners in [niche] ask most often?
    List 15 questions as potential video titles.

  > Write 3 hook variations for the same video idea: one curiosity-based,
    one problem-based, one result-based.

  > Turn this rough idea into a clear, specific title: [paste idea].
    Keep it under 10 words. No clickbait.

  > List 5 content series ideas for [niche] — each series should have
    3–5 episodes with a clear through-line.

PAGE 24 — Scripts & Outlines (10 prompts):
  > Write a 3-part outline for a [format: tutorial/story/list] video
    about [topic]. Each part should have a clear purpose and transition.

  > Turn this bullet-point outline into a flowing script for a
    [duration]-minute video. Keep sentences short. Tone: [describe].

  > Write the opening 60 seconds of a video about [topic]. Include a
    hook, a brief credibility line, and a preview of what they will learn.

  > Suggest 5 B-roll concepts I could film to support a talking-head
    video about [topic].

  > Write a mid-video CTA for [desired action] that does not feel
    salesy. Transition smoothly from the content.

  > Create a FAQ section for a video about [topic]. 5 questions the
    viewer is likely thinking but did not ask.

  > Write the closing 30 seconds of a video about [topic]. Summarize
    the key point, give one actionable next step, and end the CTA.

  > Take this transcript excerpt and tighten it — remove filler words,
    make sentences shorter, keep the voice: [paste excerpt].

  > Write a "pattern interrupt" moment for a video that is 3 minutes in
    and risks losing the viewer. Topic: [topic].

  > Outline a comparison video between [option A] and [option B] for
    someone who is [describe audience situation].

PAGE 25 — Captions & Threads (10 prompts):
  > Write a 3-sentence Instagram caption for a post about [topic].
    First sentence: bold statement. Second: expand. Third: CTA.

  > Turn this video script section into a Twitter/X thread.
    Each tweet should stand alone. 8–12 tweets. Tone: direct, useful.

  > Write a LinkedIn post about [topic] for [audience]. 150 words max.
    Lead with the insight, not the backstory.

  > Write 5 Pinterest pin descriptions for [topic]. Each under 150 chars.
    Include one keyword and one benefit.

  > Turn this blog outline into an email newsletter. 250 words max.
    Subject line options: [3 variations].

  > Write a YouTube description for a video about [topic]. Include:
    what the video covers (3 bullets), a CTA, and 5 hashtags.

  > Write a short-form video hook and caption for a [15/30/60]-second
    video about [topic]. Hook should work as the first on-screen text.

  > Rewrite this caption to be shorter and more direct.
    Remove: filler phrases, weak adjectives, any hype language. [paste]

  > Write a "results post" caption showing the outcome of [action].
    Specific numbers only. No vague claims. Platform: [platform].

  > Write 5 comment-starter captions for [niche] — posts designed to
    get the audience to share their own experience.
