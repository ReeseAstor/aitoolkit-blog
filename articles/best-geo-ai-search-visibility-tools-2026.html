What GEO Actually Is (and Why Rankings Don't Transfer)

Traditional SEO measures success in rankings and clicks. GEO measures success in citations and mentions: when a prospect asks an AI assistant a question in your category, is your brand named, how is it described, and against which competitors? The mechanics differ by platform. ChatGPT draws on training data plus its own web crawler and Bing's index, and favors pages that open with a clear definition, carry a visible author and date, and avoid marketing fluff. Perplexity runs a live search on nearly every prompt and cites generously — five to ten sources per answer — which makes it the easiest engine to earn a first citation from and the best place to test whether a page's structure works. Google AI Overviews now trigger on close to half of tracked queries by industry estimates, pulling from the traditional index but synthesizing answers instead of listing links.

Two implications follow. First, measurement is its own job: you cannot improve citation share you never see, because AI answers leave no referral trail in your analytics. Second, the content that gets cited is structurally different from the content that ranks — extractable answers, dense facts, fresh dates, entity clarity. That is why the tool market has split into three layers: pure monitoring platforms (they tell you where you stand), SEO platforms that bolted on AI visibility (they tell you where you stand and help you fix it), and the content layer that actually produces the citable material. The stack below covers all three, and for most small businesses the right move is two of the three, not all of them.

The Pure-Play GEO Monitors: Profound, Otterly.ai, and Peec AI

A fourth category exists and matters at the top of the market: dedicated AI visibility platforms built only for citation intelligence. Profound is the enterprise leader, with prompt-level tracking across the major engines, conversation-volume analytics, and features like Citation Decay that quantify week-over-week presence. Otterly.ai and Peec AI are the pure-play challengers — both monitor brand mentions, sentiment, and competitor share of voice across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Gemini, with pricing aimed at agencies and mid-market brands rather than solo operators. Semrush's AI Toolkit and Ahrefs Brand Radar fold AI citation tracking into incumbent SEO suites, which makes them the lowest-friction add-on for teams already paying for those platforms — though reviewers note their per-engine coverage runs thinner than the native GEO tools.

When do you actually need this layer? If you are a marketing agency selling GEO as a service, an in-house team at a company where AI-driven leads show up in pipeline, or a brand defending share of voice in a category where competitors are already cited — yes. If you are a small business trying to earn your first citations, quote-based enterprise pricing is premature. Your First 30 Days of GEO (No Budget Required to Start)

  1. Week 1 — Baseline: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini the 5-10 questions your buyers actually ask. Log which competitors get cited, in what language, and whether you appear at all. This is free and it is the measurement layer everything else depends on.
  2. Week 2 — Structure: take your three most important pages and make them extractable — open with a definition, add a dated FAQ section, mark up schema, and confirm the pages are crawlable. Remember: pages ChatGPT cannot access cannot be cited.
  3. Week 3 — Fact density: add statistics, named sources, and original numbers to those pages. The Princeton GEO research put up to a 40% visibility gain on exactly this change for lower-ranked sites. Refresh visible dates — the Otterly "2026 title" result (61% more citations) is a proxy for how strongly engines reward freshness.
  4. Week 4 — Systematize: pick the publishing cadence you can hold (one citable asset per week is enough), and only then decide whether a tracker — Writesonic or Surfer — is worth automating what you are still doing by hand.
